Most school science is reading about experiments someone else ran. StepUp is six months of running your own.

STAIJA started in 2023 as a weekend seminar for 15 high-school students, run by a few students who were about to start college themselves. About 100 students have been through our programs since then, and around 200 more have come to STAIJA Talks.

StepUp Scholars is the longer of our two programs: six months in Nigeria, 30 students per cohort, ages 15 to 19. If you're trying to decide whether to apply, this is roughly what you'd be signing up for.

## Month 1: learning how research works

You start with a research methods boot camp, which covers how a scientist turns a question into something they can actually test. You also get certified in lab safety before you touch any equipment.

You don't need research experience to get in. We're looking for curiosity and a willingness to learn, not a CV.

## Months 2 to 4: the actual experiment

This is the longest stretch, and it's the hands-on part. You run your experiments, collect your data, and meet with your mentor every week.

Some of it won't work. A lot of research is staring at a result that makes no sense and figuring out what to try next, and that's what the weekly check-ins are for. By the end of month 4 you have a dataset that didn't exist before you collected it.

## Month 5: making sense of it

Month 5 is analysis and writing. You work through your data, go to scientific writing workshops, and finish the month with a drafted abstract: the short paragraph at the top of a paper that says what you asked, what you did, and what you found.

## Month 6: presenting and submitting

The program ends with a symposium where you present your work. Then you submit your manuscript to youth science journals.

One honest caveat: submitted isn't the same as published. Journals decide what they take. But you'll have carried a project from a first question all the way to a finished paper, and most students never get the chance to do that.

## Time and money

Plan on about 10 hours a week for the six months. That's two hours a school day, or a couple of evenings plus a chunk of the weekend.

It costs you nothing. There's no application fee, and accepted students get a ₦50,000 monthly stipend for internet, transport, and basic needs while the program runs.

## Should you apply?

You're eligible if you're:

- 15 to 19 years old
- in secondary school or on a gap year in Nigeria
- interested in STEM, and able to show it
- able to give about 10 hours a week for six months

When we read applications, we're looking for curiosity, resilience, and room to grow. If you come from a background that doesn't usually get opportunities like this, we especially want to hear from you.

If six months in Nigeria doesn't fit, [Dynamerge](/programs/dynamerge) is our four-week virtual bootcamp, open to students anywhere in Africa. You can apply to both, but you can only do one program per calendar year.
